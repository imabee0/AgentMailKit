OBSERVED, and the detail that decides it: the resolved `pod_id` is
`133c9cbe-f996-4094-a8d5-0c6603e022ea`, which is **byte-identical to `organization_id`**. The
account's "Default Pod" is minted carrying the organization's own UUID as its pod id.

Three pods existed at the moment of the create and it chose that one — not the newest
(ae4cae42, created seconds earlier) and not the throwaway (9047724b).

Two hypotheses survive this observation and it does not discriminate between them: "the pod whose
pod_id == organization_id" and "the oldest pod" (Default Pod, 04:16Z, is also the oldest). Named
rather than glossed. The id-equality reading is the one to implement, because it is O(1), exact,
and reproducible by construction — `amk init` mints the default pod with `pod_id =
organization_id`, and then both readings agree forever in our own deployment. The "oldest pod"
reading would additionally require a tiebreak we have no evidence for.

This also explains fixture 01: an org-scoped key reports `scope_id == organization_id`.

Q2 — DELETING A NON-EMPTY POD IS REFUSED, 409, AND NOTHING CASCADES
================================================================================

POST /v0/pods  {"name":"amk-probe-podC"} -> 200   pod_id fbac6164-8b98-40f7-a6ee-98d1c665f449
POST /v0/pods/fbac6164-…/inboxes  {"username":"amkpodc"} -> 200

DELETE /v0/pods/fbac6164-8b98-40f7-a6ee-98d1c665f449 -> 409
{"name":"CannotDeleteError","code":"cannot_delete","message":"Cannot delete Pod: Cannot delete pod with existing inboxes","fix":"Pod cannot be deleted yet. Resolve the blocker described above (for example, remove dependent resources), then retry.","docs":"https://docs.agentmail.to/errors#cannot_delete"}

GET /v0/pods/fbac6164-… -> 200          (the pod survived)
GET /v0/inboxes/amkpodc%40agentmail.to -> 200   (its inbox survived)

OBSERVED: refusal is fail-closed and total — no cascade, no soft-delete, no orphan. It is the full
error envelope (app-layer), not the bare auth body, and it carries `name`/`code`/`message`/`fix`/
`docs` with no per-code extras. `message` is doubled ("Cannot delete Pod: Cannot delete pod with
existing inboxes") — reproduce the shape, not that particular prose.

**This corrects `amk-types`.** `ErrorCode::CannotDelete` mapped to **403**; the live status is
**409**. `cannot_delete` is absent from openapi.json entirely, so the 403 came from the docs page,
not the spec — and the live capture beats both.

================================================================================
DELETE STATUS CODES — openapi SAYS 200 FOR BOTH, AND BOTH ARE WRONG
================================================================================

DELETE /v0/pods/{pod_id}        -> 204   (empty pod; no body)
DELETE /v0/inboxes/{inbox_id}   -> 202   (accepted; deletion is asynchronous)

`openapi.json` documents `200` for each. Two divergences the conformance diff would have caught
only after the handlers were written. 202 on the inbox path is the more consequential of the two:
it says inbox deletion is accepted-then-processed, not completed-on-return, so a test that deletes
an inbox and immediately asserts 404 on GET is racing the server.

Corroborates B5 independently of fixture 18: `limit_exceeded` is **403** (not 429), the quota is
counted **organization-wide** (podB was empty; the org held 3 inboxes), and the extras are
`resource`/`limit`/`upgrade_url`. AgentMailKit reproduces `resource` and `limit`, and omits
`upgrade_url` and the price-bearing `fix` prose — the no-billing-surface rule, applied on purpose.

Same rule for `type_organizations:Organization`: it carries `billing_id`, `billing_type` and
`billing_subscription_id`. We emit none of the three. That is a deliberate divergence from 1:1 and
the conformance diff must be told so rather than "fixed".
